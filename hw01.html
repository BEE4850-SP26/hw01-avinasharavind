Based on the simulation results, the skeptic’s hypothesis does not appear very plausible. Create a generative model for the grant-selection procedure under the null assumption of no correlation between rigor and impact. Plot a histogram of the correlations between rigor and data for (a) the funded proposals and (b) the overall pool. What do you observe?

Among the funded applications in Figure 5, all have a negative rigor-impact correlation, with the peak of the sample distribution at around -0.8, thus confirming the researcher’s findings of a negative correlation for the funded proposals. However, the researcher’s claim was that all high-impact proposals were necessarily less rigorous, which would imply a similar negative correlation among all proposals. The sample distribution in Figure 6 is nearly zero-centered and is visually similar to the Normal distribution, suggesting there is no strong correlation between rigor and impact among the general pool of proposals, suggesting the researcher’s claim is incorrect.

Problem 4.2

Explain why, when conditioning on selection for funding, there might be a negative correlation between rigor and impact even if there none generally exists.

Since both rigor and impact scores are drawn from a normally distributed population, there will be some proposals that score highly on both metrics, some that score poorly on both, some that score highly on one but not the other, and some that score near the mean on one or both. As such, the correlation between rigor and impact across any set of 200 funding proposals will depend on exactly how many proposals fall into the above categories; that is, it will look relatively random, explaining why Figure 6 looks similar to a normal distribution. However, when controlling for only the top 10% of total scores in each set, the proposals that scored poorly on either metric are likely to be removed from the subset, leaving only proposals that did strongly on both or strongly on one and near-average on another. For most of these proposals, one metric performed better than the other, and so amongst the set of funded proposals, an increase in rigor appears to be correlated with a decrease in rigor and vice-versa. Thus, the correlation amongst funded proposals is likely to be negative, leading to the distribution in Figure 5.
